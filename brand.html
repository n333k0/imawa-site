04

Social

Sized for each placement and ready to upload. Instagram and LinkedIn take the same square avatar.

Avatars — 1080 × 1080

On small avatars. The wordmark is fine and wide, so at the ~40px a feed renders it becomes faint. Black is the primary because it holds up best. If the mark ever needs to read at thumbnail size, that is a monogram decision for the brand owner, not something to improvise here.

LinkedIn covers — 1584 × 396

Why the content sits off to the right. LinkedIn lays the profile photo over the lower-left of the cover, roughly 260px wide, and crops the edges further on phones. Everything here starts past that, so nothing important disappears behind the avatar.

Post templates — 1080 × 1080

The third is a frame, not a finished post: drop a still or artwork behind it and keep the dark band so the mark stays legible.

Installing it

  • Gmail. Settings → See all settings → Signature → paste into the box. Paste the rendered block above, not the code, and Gmail keeps the layout.
  • Apple Mail. Settings → Signatures → paste, then untick "Always match my default message font".
  • Outlook. Paste into the signature editor. Outlook on Windows may add its own spacing around tables; the layout holds.

06

Usage

Short on purpose. Six rules cover almost everything.

  • Give it room. Keep clear space around the mark of at least the height of its own letters. It is a fine wordmark and crowding kills it.
  • Do not redraw it. No stretching, no outlining, no shadows, no gradients, and never retype it in another font — the letterforms are custom.
  • One accent. Cream is for labels, rules and small emphasis. Headlines stay paper or black.
  • Contrast over placement. On artwork, put the mark on a dark scrim rather than hunting for a quiet corner.
  • Black is the default ground. Paper and cream are alternates, not equals.
  • Type is Light. Plus Jakarta Sans at 300 for display and body; Medium only for small uppercase labels.
